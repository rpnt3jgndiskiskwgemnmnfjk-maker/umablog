## In my view

August 11 looked like a single risk-off day, but it was really two unconnected worries landing on the market at once: a geopolitical oil shock from the stalled Strait of Hormuz talks, and a valuation reckoning in AI-heavy tech names led by Alphabet. Both pushed the Nasdaq and S&P 500 lower and both got framed by traders as "waiting on Wednesday's CPI," but CPI could only ever settle the inflation half of that equation. My read is that the rebound the next day, once July CPI came in as expected, only cleared the macro overhang — it did nothing to resolve either the Hormuz standoff or the deeper question of whether hyperscaler capex is outrunning AI returns. Both of those threads look likely to resurface as independent shocks later this quarter, not as a single story that CPI could neatly close out.

## The evidence

U.S. stocks fell for a second straight session on Tuesday: the Dow slipped about 0.3%, the S&P 500 lost roughly 0.3%, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped closer to 0.6%, according to Reuters and Yahoo Finance market wraps. Bloomberg's live blog attributed the pressure to Brent crude pushing above $89 a barrel as a U.S.-Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz remained deadlocked; Iran's Foreign Ministry said Washington would need to lift its blockade first, while separate crypto-market coverage from CoinDesk and FXStreet linked the latest leg of the selloff to reports that Trump was pressing Iran for decades of compensation, a demand that reportedly killed near-term hopes of a reopening deal. Energy, utilities, and industrials were among the day's few gainers even as growth and tech names dragged the index down — a rotation Bloomberg described explicitly as money moving out of high-valuation stocks into defensive and cyclical ones.

Alphabet was the clearest casualty of that rotation, falling roughly 3.4-3.6% according to TradingKey's market-mover data, on top of an already-jittery narrative around 2026 capital-expenditure guidance of $195-205 billion, a quarterly capex jump of 100% year-over-year, and free cash flow that had turned negative — compounded by reports of leadership churn inside its AI unit and ongoing antitrust scrutiny of its ad business.

Bitcoin traded as low as roughly $63,850 and struggled to hold $64,000, per CoinDesk, squeezed by the same oil-driven inflation anxiety plus reported selling pressure from Strategy. One notable outlier: the NFIB Small Business Optimism Index actually rose to 99.8, its best reading since August 2025, a Main Street bright spot that got little attention against the Wall Street gloom.

The next morning, July CPI landed exactly at consensus — up 0.1% for the month, 3.4% year-over-year — and stocks rallied close to record levels, per Bloomberg. That relief, though, addressed only the inflation-rate anxiety; the Hormuz impasse and the AI-capex debate were still exactly where they'd been on August 11.
