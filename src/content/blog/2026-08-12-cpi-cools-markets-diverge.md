## In my view

The headline story on August 12 — soft CPI, record stocks — is true but incomplete. Equities were pricing a narrow, Fed-specific relief: less chance of a September hike, plus an AI-earnings tailwind. Bonds, gold, the dollar, and bitcoin were each pricing something the CPI print didn't resolve — sticky longer-run inflation risk in one case, an unsettled Strait of Hormuz standoff in the other. I'd treat the record close as a read on rate-hike odds, not as evidence the broader risk backdrop actually cleared.

## The rally, and why it happened

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' July report showed headline CPI up 0.1% on the month (3.4% year-over-year) and core CPI up 0.2% (2.5% year-over-year), matching the slowest annual pace since March 2021, according to Bloomberg's coverage. Every component matched consensus. Falling oil and grocery prices did the heavy lifting, per CNBC's markets recap. Traders responded by trimming the odds of a September Fed hike to roughly 40%, per CME FedWatch data cited by CNBC, with close to 60% now expecting the Fed to hold at 3.50%–3.75%. The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,798.99, up 0.65% and above 7,800 intraday for the first time, while the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.81%. CNBC credited both the cooler inflation reading and falling oil prices; Bloomberg added that strong results from CoreWeave and Super Micro Computer — shares up as much as 24% and 18% respectively — reinforced confidence in the AI trade that same session.

## The bond market's own newsletter disagreed

Even as stocks celebrated, Bloomberg published a newsletter that same day headlined "Bonds, Dollar and Gold Are Flashing an Inflation Warning," arguing that rising yields, a firmer gold price, and a softer dollar together suggested investors still expect inflation to run above the Fed's target over the longer stretch — a read that sits uneasily next to the "cooling inflation" framing driving equities. Both pieces of Bloomberg's own coverage ran on August 12; they simply emphasized different time horizons.

## Bitcoin kept falling

Crypto ignored the equity rally. Japanese exchange bitbank's August 12 commentary, titled (translated) "BTC continues to decline, watching U.S. CPI and the Middle East situation," described bitcoin falling for a second straight session, swinging between roughly ¥10.08 million and ¥10.26 million as headlines about the Strait of Hormuz flip-flopped — a brief lift on reported ceasefire progress cited by Pakistan's defense minister, then a pullback after Iran reiterated it wouldn't reopen the strait without US concessions. Bitbank's own framing tied the move directly to that unresolved standoff rather than to the day's Fed-friendly data. Tokyo's Nikkei 225, meanwhile, added roughly 255 points to around 67,225 in afternoon trading, per Minkabu — a gain built on individual stock strength ahead of, not in reaction to, the evening's US CPI release, since Tokyo's session closes before the data lands.
