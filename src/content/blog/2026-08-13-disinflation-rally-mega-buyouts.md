August 13, 2026 looked, at first glance, like just another record-high day for U.S. stocks. Underneath it was a more interesting story: falling inflation didn't just lift share prices, it appears to have reopened the door to the kind of mega leveraged buyout that had been mostly dormant.

## The inflation-to-rally pipeline

The chain of events started a day earlier, when the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reported July CPI rose just 0.1% month-over-month, holding the annual rate at 3.4%, in line with consensus. On the 13th, the follow-through arrived: the Producer Price Index for July came in flat, with core PPI up only 0.2%, slightly below the 0.3% economists had expected, according to CNBC. Together, the two reports pushed traders to further pare bets on a Federal Reserve rate hike — money markets priced in less than a 40% chance of a September move, per Bloomberg.

Equities responded accordingly. The S&P 500 closed above 7,800 for the first time, up 0.65% on the day, while the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.81% to 26,803.03, lifted by Meta, Micron, and Netflix, Reuters reported. In Tokyo, the Nikkei 225 notched a third straight day of gains, closing up 1.16% at 68,308.59 as AI and semiconductor names led buying, according to Minkabu, while the yen stayed soft near 159.40–159.50 against the dollar amid lingering intervention chatter.

## Cheaper money, bigger deals

What made the day distinctive was what falling yields did to deal-making. Reuters reported that private equity firm Silver Lake is in talks to take Workday private in a deal that could value the enterprise-software company north of $43 billion — among the largest software buyouts ever attempted. Workday shares surged as much as 21% and were briefly halted for volatility, closing near $206.

The same pattern played out at Wendy's. Nelson Peltz's Trian Fund Management, which already holds roughly a 16% stake, was reported to be assembling a take-private consortium alongside Wendy's franchisee Flynn Group and Abu Dhabi's BlueFive Capital. The stock, still reeling from a rough second quarter that saw same-restaurant sales fall 7% and its dividend cut in half, jumped as much as 15% on the report and was also halted for volatility. Forbes JAPAN flagged the same move in its own coverage the same morning.

Two unrelated companies, two buyout rumors, one afternoon — the common thread is financing cost. As Treasury yields fell alongside the softer inflation prints, the math behind leveraged buyouts got easier, and sponsors sitting on dry powder moved.

Not every asset joined the party: bitbank's daily desk note described bitcoin as unable to extend gains despite the cooler CPI, drifting without clear direction even as equities broke records — a reminder that risk appetite didn't broaden evenly across markets that day.

## My own read

In my view — and this is interpretation, not confirmed reporting — the Workday and Wendy's deals landing on the same day as a record S&P close is a useful signal that the rally by mid-August had shifted from a purely AI-earnings story toward a broader "cheap financing" story. If PPI and CPI keep printing soft into September, I'd expect more private equity activity to surface, not less, precisely because two of 2026's more troubled large-cap names just got bid up on nothing more than buyout speculation. Whether that appetite survives a hawkish surprise from the Fed is the open question worth watching next.
