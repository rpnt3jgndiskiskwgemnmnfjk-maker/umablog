## In my view

The headline story out of August 13 looks like a clean "disinflation unlocks risk appetite" narrative, but the details argue for something more selective. Equities — especially AI and semiconductor names in both the US and Japan — did the celebrating, while the bond and crypto markets stayed skeptical. A 30-year Treasury auction that cleared at its highest yield in a quarter century and a Bitcoin slide that continued right through the CPI print both suggest investors are drawing a distinction between "the Fed won't hike" and "everything is fine." That's a narrower, more fragile rally than the record index levels alone would suggest.

The trigger was July's US Consumer Price Index, released the prior day and still driving trading on August 13: headline inflation rose 0.1% month-over-month to a 3.4% annual pace, with core CPI up 0.2% and 2.5% year-over-year — in line with consensus. Bloomberg's markets wrap described the reaction plainly: traders "drove stocks higher and bond yields fell as more evidence of moderating inflation" reinforced bets the Fed would hold off on any rate hike, with the S&P 500 setting a fresh record and the Nasdaq 100 adding roughly 1.15%. Money markets were pricing under a 40% chance of a September hike.

Japan caught the same tailwind. According to Minkabu's market close report, the Nikkei 225 rose for a third straight session, gaining 784.53 points (1.16%) to close at 68,308.59, with AI and semiconductor issues doing the heavy lifting after the CPI reading eased US rate-hike fears and a strong Korean KOSPI added to the tone. Profit-taking narrowed the gain somewhat by the close, an early sign that even the biggest winners weren't chasing without limits.

Where the "all clear" reading breaks down is in two other corners of the market. Bloomberg also flagged that the US sold 30-year bonds at the highest yield in 25 years the same week — a signal that long-term investors still want heavy compensation to finance the deficit, tame CPI or not. And in crypto, bitbank's daily Bitcoin analysis for August 13 noted BTC had fallen for three consecutive days and dropped below its Ichimoku cloud, a technically bearish signal, even as the softer CPI reading gave equities a lift. Bitcoin had briefly touched roughly ¥10.24 million after the print but slipped back to around ¥10.11 million, essentially flat, with resistance confirmed at higher levels.

Separately, Forbes Japan's coverage of copper — which touched a fresh record near $6.80 a pound on tightening Chinese-controlled supply — is a reminder that some of this month's records are structural commodity stories tied to AI infrastructure demand, not just a Fed-driven rate trade. Taken together, the picture for August 13 is less "markets breathe a sigh of relief" and more "the assets already leaning on an AI and rate-cut narrative got the confirmation they wanted, while bonds and crypto stayed unconvinced."
